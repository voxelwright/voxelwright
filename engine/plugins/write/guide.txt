world.write({ask, for, rule})   asks for a rule of this world to be changed, or a new one written, in words: ask says what should change or be added (up to 600 characters: "the agents now climb walls after whoever flies", "add a market at the north end where the stall-keepers haggle"); for is the id in world.people of someone at a page (kind "page"): it is written by that person's own model on their own machine, tried in a world of its own, and put in only if it runs, so it is for the one whose game it changes; rule is the id of the rule to change, one that is there (left out: the realm's own rule, the one written for its idea; in a world with none, a new rule); not "talk" or "director". It takes minutes, and of the writing itself nothing is heard back: go on meanwhile, and do not count on it. At most once in 5 minutes for one person and for one rule, and an ask for someone whose page has no helper is used up all the same and writes nothing. A rule cannot ask for itself to be rewritten. This is how something that watches the play (a director, a game master) makes the game itself grow: use it for what your own actors and marks cannot do
