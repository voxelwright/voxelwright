world.ground(id, {at, within, grid})   asks for the lie of the land over a square round at: [x, z], reaching within cells each way (300 unless said, up to 4000; a cell is 10 cm): ask before building anything big, to know where there is room, which way the ground slopes and where the water is. The answer comes on this rule's next call, as 'ground'. grid is how many columns a side are told (9 unless said, up to 17). id is this rule's own name for the question
world.ground(id, {find, near, room, flat, within})   asks where the nearest ground of a kind is, with room to build on: find is "open" (open country, outside the town), "grass", "sand" (the dry plain east of the town, and beaches), "rock", "shore" (low land with the sea beside it), "street" (a road in the town), "high" (mountains) or "water" (the sea). near: [x, z] is where to look from. room is how far round the place the ground must be clear, with nothing standing on it and no water (300 cells unless said, up to 3000: half the width of what you mean to build). flat is the most the ground may rise across that room, in cells (a sixth of room unless said, all of room for high and rock: ask for less to build something level, and expect to look further for it). within is how far to look, in cells (200000 unless said, which is 20 km; up to 400000). Every realm is the same planet: a town in the middle, a dry sandy plain from 40000 cells (4 km) east of it, mountains from 50000 cells north, the sea 60000 cells west with lakes and inlets nearer, grass country between. So the right ground for a place is often kilometres off: leave within out, build where the answer says and tell people where it is
  on('ground', (world, e) => {})   the answer. To at: e = {ground (the id), at: [x, y, z], within, step (cells between columns), low, high (the lowest and highest ground: high - low is how much it slopes), water (how much of it is sea, 0 to 1), clear (how much is land with nothing standing on it), town ("homes", "towers", "park", "yard", "shore" or null in open country), most (what most of the ground is made of), h, of, up}. h, of and up are the grid, north row first, each row from west to east: h[row][column] is the height someone stands at there (under water, the height of the bed: the water's surface is at height 0 everywhere, so a negative h is how deep it is); of[row] is a string with a letter a column for what the ground is: g grass, s sand, r rock, e earth, a asphalt (a road), c concrete (pavement), w water, x something built; up[row][column] is how high what stands there reaches over the ground, 0 where it is clear (a wall, a car, a tree's trunk; 40 means that or more). Column i of row j is at x = at[0] - within + i * step, z = at[2] - within + j * step
  To find: e = {ground, find, found: true, at: [x, y, z] (the middle of the place), far (cells from near), room, rise (how much the ground rises across the room), of, town, water: {to: "west" | "north-west" ..., far} (for a shore: which way the sea lies and how far)}, or {ground, find, found: false, why}. Keep the answer in world.keep and build there: world.build(id, {at: [e.at[0], e.at[2]], parts}). A rule asks about the ground at most 4 times a second and looks for ground once a second
