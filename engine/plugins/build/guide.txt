world.build(id, {at, parts})   builds a place out of the world's own cells: walls, floors, ramps, fences, a ring with ropes, a stand, a track, a trench, a bunker. It is real: everyone sees and walks on it, it breaks, burns and falls, and is kept. at: [x, z] is the place its parts are measured from; a cell is 10 cm, x is east, z south, y up. parts is a list of up to 200, laid in order (a later part replaces an earlier one where they meet); each is a list
  ["box", material, colour, x0, y0, z0, x1, y1, z1]   a level block from a low corner to a high one (high corner not filled). y is over the ground at at; a box whose y0 is 0 is carried down to the ground where it falls away
  ["wedge", material, colour, x0, y0, z0, x1, y1, z1, falls]   a ramp filling that box, sloping down towards falls: "+x", "-x", "+z" or "-z". ["steps", ..., falls, rise] is the same in steps rise cells high (2 for stairs, 4 for the rows of a grandstand). Either may end with thick: only that many cells under its top are filled
  ["line", material, colour, [[x, z], ...], wide, high, lift]   a band along 2 to 64 points, lying on the ground under each column, so it follows rolling land: a road or a track (wide 60 to 120, high 4, lift -4: it replaces the ground's top cells; thinner, it shows in stripes from far off), a wall (wide 3, high 20), a rail or a rope (wide 1, high 1, lift 8), a trench ("air", wide 12, high 15, lift -15). lift is how far over the ground it starts (0 if left out). ["loop", ...] is the same, closed: a circuit
  ["ring", material, colour, x, z, radius, wide, high, lift]   a round band on the ground, radius to its middle. ["disc", material, colour, x, z, radius, high, lift] is a round patch
  ["posts", material, colour, [[x, z], ...], wide, high, lift]   a square post at each of up to 64 points, each on its own ground: fence posts, pillars, obstacles, boxes
  material is earth, rock, grass, sand, asphalt, concrete, brick, plaster, wood, glass, steel, leaves, roofing, lamp (it glows), or air, which cuts: out of what this build laid before it and out of the ground. colour is "own" (the material's), "white", "black", "grey:0" to "grey:14", or "hue:tone": hue 0 red, 2 orange, 4 yellow, 8 green, 12 cyan, 16 blue, 18 violet, 20 magenta; tone 0 palest, 3 mid, 4 vivid, 6 dark, 8 dusty. So ["posts", "steel", "0:4", [[-30, -30], [30, -30], [30, 30], [-30, 30]], 2, 14] is four red corner posts 1.4 m high, and ["loop", "steel", "white", [[-30, -30], [30, -30], [30, 30], [-30, 30]], 1, 1, 12] a white rope round them
  What is built must rest on something or it falls: a rail or a rope needs a post every 8 m or less, a roof needs walls. Ground is not level (several metres over 100 m): lines, rings, discs and posts follow it. People are 18 cells tall and step up 2: a higher floor needs a ramp or steps
  A build is whole or not at all. It may reach 3000 cells from at, be 1,000,000 cells and 60,000 rows (a row runs east to west: tall things running north to south cost most). Nothing is built into or under someone at a page (it waits until they step away: {build, waits}), or over another rule's build (over this rule's own earlier build, the cells become the later build's). A rule has 16 builds at most and changes them 12 times a minute at most
  Asked again with the same id, at and parts, nothing is done: so build on 'start' whatever start is told. With the same id and other parts, the old build is taken away and the new one built (a gate opens by building it again without its bar)
world.build(id, {clear: true})   takes the build away and puts back what it replaced (not where it was broken since, or someone stands). So does taking the rule out
  on('built', (world, e) => {})   what came of a build: e = {build (its id), cells, at: [x, y, z] (y: the ground's height boxes were measured from), low: [x, y, z], high: [x, y, z] (the box round it)}, or {build, failed: "why"} (nothing was built), or {build, cleared: true}. An actor placed after this stands on top of it
