world.look(id, {from, to})   asks what the world itself puts between two places: a wall, a parked car, a tree. from and to are each a place [x, y, z], or the id of one of this rule's actors or of someone in world.people (then it is from, or to, their eyes). The answer comes on this rule's next call, as 'seen'. A look meets what the world is made of, never people (world.people says where they are), and goes at most 800 cells. id is this rule's own name for the look: lower-case letters, digits and dashes. Use it to know whether one person can see another, whether a straight walk is blocked, where cover is
world.look(id, {from, round: true})   the same, level, the eight ways round from a place or from someone's eyes: for finding the open way, or the nearest wall to stand behind
  on('seen',  (world, e) => {})   the answer to a look this rule asked for: e = {look (its id), from: [x, y, z], to: [x, y, z], clear (true if nothing is between), far (cells to what was met, or to the place if clear), at: [x, y, z] and material ("brick", "steel", "wood", "glass", "leaves" ...) of what was met}. For a look round: e = {look, from, round: [{to: "north" | "north-east" | ... , far, material (null if nothing within 800 cells)} for the eight ways]}. North is -z, east is +x. A rule looks at most 32 times a second (a look round is eight): look when something changes, not every tick for everyone
