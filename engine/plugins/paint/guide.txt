world.paint(id, {at, within, line, closed, colour, wide, fill})   draws a mark on the ground that everyone there sees, as chalk on a street: a ring, at: [x, z] its middle and within: cells to the ring (1 to 2000), or a line, line: [[x, z], ...] of 2 to 32 points (at most 4000 cells end to end each way), closed: true to join its last point to its first (a box, a court, a lane). colour is "#rrggbb" (chalk white, "#f4f1e8", unless said); wide is how wide the line is, 1 to 40 cells (3: 30 cm); fill is 0 to 0.8, how much the inside of a ring or a closed line is tinted with its colour (0 unless said). Given again, only the fields given change (a line given takes the place of a ring, and a ring of a line); hide: true takes it away. id: lower-case letters, digits and dashes, up to 24. A mark lies on what is level and near the ground under it (the road, a pavement, a low step), goes under a parked car and is hidden behind a house; it is never drawn on a wall or a person. It is only seen: it is no cells of the world, stops nobody and is heard by no rule. For who is inside it, world.mark the same place (at and within) and hear near. Use it to show where a game is played, a start or a finish line, a goal, a stall's patch, a way to go; move a ring with someone by drawing it again where they are. A rule has at most 16 marks and a world 64, and a rule changes its marks at most 20 times a second (drawing one as it already is costs nothing). The marks go when the rule is taken out, and are not kept when the world starts again: draw them in start, whatever its why
