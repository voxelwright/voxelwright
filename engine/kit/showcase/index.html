Voxelmorphism

Skeuomorphism painted controls to look like leather and brushed metal. Voxelmorphism builds them out of blocks. Every control on this page is a small solid made of voxels, lit by one light, and you can count what it is made of.

Poke the sign, or hold and brush it. It mends itself.

The rules

The voxel is the unit

One voxel is 4 px. Every size is a whole number of voxels: a button is 11 tall, a switch is 16 by 8, a gap is 1. Nothing is a fraction of a block, so nothing looks nearly right.

Change the size of a voxel and the whole interface scales with it, the way a brick wall scales with its bricks.

The front face sits on the pixel grid

There is no camera. The view is a cabinet projection: the face of every voxel is a square of whole pixels, and height shows as a shift of half a voxel down and to the right, as if you stood up and to the left of the page.

So text stays sharp, a control is where its box says it is, and depth is still real depth.

Height says what you can do

A thing that stands proud of the page can be pressed, and it goes down when you press it. A well cut into a slab takes what you type. A thing that sits low is switched off. The page itself is bedrock: nothing goes below it.

Material says what it is for

Colour is never chosen for looks. Enamel is the thing to press. Signal is where you are and what is on, and it glows at night. Chalk carries content. Slate is the floor of a well or a track. Steel is switched off. Walnut carries other things. Leaf has gone well and brick has not.

One light, and real shadows

The light comes from the top left, as it has in interfaces since the first bevelled button. Tops are brightest, fronts are the true colour, sides are darkest. Shadows are cast, not painted, so they tighten when a button goes down.

Dark mode is night. The materials stay; the light drops, and anything made of signal lights the wall around it.

Things are built, and they break

When something is made, it is assembled in front of you: lowest layer first, each block set down from your side of the page. When it is removed, it crumbles and falls. A break is quicker than a build.

This marks making and unmaking only. Nothing assembles because you scrolled to it, and nothing moves while you read.

The kit

Eleven parts, each a real HTML control underneath. Tab through them: the keyboard works as it would with no voxels at all.

A plant, grown from a number

The growth meter is a plant. Every voxel in it has a birthday between 0 and 1, so one number draws it at any moment of its life: seed, sprout, sapling, bud, bloom. Each plant grows from its own seed number and no two are alike.

This is the heart of Plant a Seed, the first app built with the kit.

The plain version

The voxels are an addition. Underneath, every part is ordinary HTML styled by one stylesheet, and that plain version is what you get with no script or no WebGL2. Turn Voxels off at the top of the page to see it.

If you ask your system for less motion, you keep the voxels and lose the travel: nothing flies in or falls away, states change with a short fade, and the sign holds still.

Use it

Link the stylesheet, write ordinary markup with the class names, and call adopt(). There is no build step and no framework. A part of your own is a function that fills a model with boxes.

The rules in full, with every number, are in the spec.

<link rel="stylesheet" href="voxelmorphism.css">

<button class="vx-button">Plant a seed</button>

<script type="module">
  import { adopt } from './voxelmorphism/src/index.js';
  adopt();
</script>

Voxelmorphism is the design language of Plant a Seed. Type is Overpass; the lettering in voxels is Vox Sign, drawn for this.
