In use: a post on the fogline blog

Last spring a deploy of harbor-api took eleven minutes, and nobody could say why. This is what we found when we went looking, and what we changed.

Where the time went

Most of it was not the build. The build took 45 seconds. The rest was waiting: for a runner, for a cache that was never warm, and for a health check that asked the wrong question of a service that had been ready for minutes.

A deploy that takes eleven minutes is a deploy people put off until Friday.

Mina, in the review

What we changed

  • The cache is keyed on the lockfile, not on the branch.
  • The health check asks /ready, which answers as soon as the service can take traffic.
  • Runners stay warm during working hours.

What it cost

Two afternoons, and one evening when the warm runners would not go cold again. A deploy now takes under two minutes. Ada wrote up the runner change in a note of its own.

Times are medians over the thirty deploys before and after the change.
