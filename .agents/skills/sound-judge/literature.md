## 1. Loudness and level

- K-weighting stage 1 (head-shelf), ITU-R BS.1770. pyloudnorm meter.py: high shelf gain +4.0 dB, Q 0.707, "fc 1500 Hz"; stage 2 high-pass Q 0.5, fc 38 Hz. https://github.com/csteinmetz1/pyloudnorm/blob/master/pyloudnorm/meter.py. FETCHED. CAUTION: I recall pyloudnorm using fc = 1681.97 Hz (G 3.99984, Q 0.70718) and HP fc 38.135 Hz (Q 0.50033); the 1500 figure in the fetch summary conflicts, so do not trust it; use the 48 kHz coefficients below. MEMORY for 1681.97.
- BS.1770 48 kHz biquad coefficients (direct form, a0=1). Stage 1: b = [1.53512485958697, -2.69169618940638, 1.19839281085285], a = [-1.69065929318241, 0.73248077421585]. Stage 2 (RLB): b = [1, -2, 1], a = [-1.99004745483398, 0.99007225036621]. MEMORY (high confidence; verify by measuring a 997 Hz sine: a mono full-scale sine should read about -3.0 LUFS).
- Loudness formula: block loudness l = -0.691 + 10 log10(sum_i G_i z_i), z_i = mean square of K-weighted channel i over block; G = 1 for L, R, C and 1.41 (about +1.5 dB) for Ls, Rs. pyloudnorm, as above. FETCHED. Consequence: a full-scale 997 Hz sine in one channel measures about -3.01 LUFS (mean square 0.5, -0.691 + 10log10(0.5) = -3.70; K-weighting gain at 1 kHz is about +0.69 dB, net -3.01). MEMORY for the K gain at 1 kHz.
- Gating: blocks 400 ms, 75% overlap (step 100 ms); absolute gate -70 LKFS; relative gate = (loudness of blocks above absolute gate) - 10 LU; integrated = loudness of blocks above both gates. pyloudnorm (block_size 0.400, overlap 0.75, relative gate -10). FETCHED (same URL).
- True peak: oversample (BS.1770-4 Annex 2 gives 4x for 48 kHz material, interpolation via polyphase FIR) then take max |x|, report dBTP. MEMORY. Practical: scipy.signal.resample_poly(x, 4, 1).
- EBU R128: programme loudness -23 LUFS, tolerance +/-0.5 LU (+/-1 LU for live), max true peak -1 dBTP; no LRA ceiling mandated. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBU_R_128. FETCHED.
- LRA (EBU Tech 3342): short-term loudness (3 s window, sampled every 100 ms) -> absolute gate -70 LUFS -> relative gate -20 LU below mean of the absolute-gated values -> LRA = 95th percentile minus 10th percentile (LU). Search snippets confirm "difference between the estimated 10th and 95th percentile", "-70 LUFS absolute threshold", and a diagram labelled -20 LU; a Rust implementation (ebur128-stream lra.rs) states 3 s window every 100 ms. https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3342.pdf (PDF unreadable via fetch; the fetch model offered the same numbers from its memory). SNIPPET + MEMORY. Note: 2011 revision changed the integrated relative gate -8 -> -10 LU (SNIPPET); LRA gate stayed -20.
- Game targets. Sony ASWG-R001: -24 LKFS +/-2 LU home consoles, -18 LKFS +/-2 LU portable, measured over at least 30 min of representative play. SNIPPET (ITU-R BS.2054 report and Audiokinetic text). A 2013 Sony slide gave -23 LUFS (SNIPPET), so version matters. https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BS.2054-4-2014-PDF-E.pdf (not opened).
- G.A.N.G.-style IESD recommendation: integrated -23 LUFS +/-2, BS.1770-3, >=30 min of play, true peak not above -1 dBFS; Vita -18 LUFS; monitor at 79 dB SPL for home vs 85 theatrical. https://designingsound.org/2013/02/loudness-in-game-audio/ . FETCHED. No dialogue/music/SFX relative levels there.
- Streaming normalisation targets: Spotify -14 LUFS (premium Loud -11 with limiter 5 ms attack/100 ms decay, Quiet -19); true peak at or below -1 dBTP, or -2 dBTP if master louder than -14. https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/ FETCHED. Wikipedia Loudness war table: Apple Music -16, YouTube -14, SoundCloud -14, Tidal -14, Amazon -13, YouTube Music -7 (odd; verify). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudness_war FETCHED.
- Dynamics history: 1980s average rock RMS about -16.8 dBFS; 1995 Morning Glory about -8 dBFS RMS; Deruty & Tardieu (JAES 62(1/2) 42-55, 2014): crest factor fell significantly 1985-2010 while LRA stayed about constant (macrodynamics kept); the 1967-84 era trended high-fidelity, 1984-2004 trended to transient degradation. Wikipedia (FETCHED) and search abstract (SNIPPET: https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=17085). Their actual numbers not obtained (JSTOR, HAL blocked). Deruty & Pachet 2015 ISMIR (https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/136_Paper.pdf) use PRRC and say the trend peaked about 2007: SNIPPET.
- Rule-of-thumb PLR and LRA by genre (peak-to-loudness ratio = true peak dBTP minus integrated LUFS): loud pop/rock/EDM masters about 6-10 dB PLR, LRA 3-7 LU; acoustic/jazz about 12-16 dB PLR, LRA 6-12; orchestral 16-25 dB PLR, LRA 12-20+. MEMORY, no measured source found. Use as sanity bands only. A game music stem at -14 LUFS with PLR under 6 dB is hyper-compressed.

## 2. Spectral balance

- Pink noise = -3 dB/octave on a power spectral density per-Hz basis (equal energy per octave; -10 dB/decade). Flat on a 1/3-octave or per-octave analyser. MEMORY (definition).
- Pestana, Ma, Reiss, Barbosa, Black, "Spectral characteristics of popular commercial recordings 1950-2010", AES 135 (2013), paper 8960. Found: long-term contours averaged over a large dataset, a novel averaging method, a consistent drift toward a common target EQ curve; per-octave slope and HF roll-off numbers NOT obtained (AES 403, copy at https://joshreiss.github.io/documents/2013/Pestana%20Ma%20Reiss%20-%20AES135.pdf downloaded as binary). Abstract: SNIPPET. Numbers: unknown. My recollection that commercial music has a low-frequency peak near 50-100 Hz, then a fall of roughly 4-6 dB/octave above about 1 kHz, steeper than pink, is MEMORY and unverified; derive the target from your own reference set instead.
- Sound-design practice: keep per-octave band energy within about +/-3 dB of a smoothed reference curve, large dips of more than 6 dB broad are audible as holes. MEMORY / practice, no source.
- Conventional band names (Owsinski/Senior/SoS tables), MEMORY, not fetched (SoS page 410): sub 20-60 Hz; bass 60-250; mud/boom 200-500 (often quoted 200-400); boxiness 300-600 (cardboard tone, 400-800 for some texts); honk 700-1.5k; presence 2-5 kHz; harshness/edge 2-5 kHz (ear canal resonance region of highest sensitivity 2-4 kHz); sibilance 5-9 kHz (female 6-9, male 4-7); brightness 6-10 k; air 10-16+ kHz. Check 200-500 Hz bump over smoothed spectrum greater than 3 dB as the "mud" detector.

## 3. Psychoacoustics

- Bark (Zwicker & Terhardt): z = 13 atan(0.00076 f) + 3.5 atan((f/7500)^2). Traunmuller 1990: z = 26.81 f/(1960+f) - 0.53. Wang-Sekey-Gersho: z = 6 asinh(f/600). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bark_scale FETCHED. Critical bandwidth table: 24 bands from 20 Hz; 100 Hz wide up to 500 Hz, then 110 (400-510), 120, 140, 150, 160 (920-1080), 190, ... 280 (1.72-2.0k), 380 (2.32-2.7k), 700 (3.7-4.4k), 1100 (5.3-6.4k), 2500 (9.5-12k), 3500 (12-15.5k). FETCHED (same).
- ERB (Glasberg & Moore 1990): ERB(f) = 24.7 (4.37 f + 1) with f in kHz, result in Hz (the fetch tool wrote kHz; the form 24.7(4.37 f/1000 + 1) Hz is standard); ERB-rate E = 21.4 log10(1 + 4.37 f_kHz). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalent_rectangular_bandwidth FETCHED. At 100 Hz ERB is about 35.5 Hz, at 1 kHz about 132.6 Hz.
- Sethares' parameterisation of Plomp-Levelt (his MATLAB dissmeasure.m): Dstar = 0.24, S1 = 0.0207, S2 = 18.96, C1 = 5, C2 = -5, A1 = -3.51, A2 = -5.75; s = Dstar / (S1 f_min + S2); pair dissonance d = a_i a_j [C1 exp(A1 s |f2-f1|) + C2 exp(A2 s |f2-f1|)] i.e. exp(-3.51 s df) minus exp(-5.75 s df) with weights 5 and -5 (the book/paper rounds exponent to b1 = 3.5, b2 = 5.75, x* = 0.24 so the constants you gave are the paper's; code uses 3.51). https://sethares.engr.wisc.edu/comprog.html (constants line read by fetch; corroborated by search snippet of the same page). Amplitude term: originally product a1*a2, later changed to min(a1, a2) (endolith gist https://gist.github.com/endolith/3066664, FETCHED description). Total dissonance sums over all partial pairs and halves. Max dissonance at df about 0.24 of the critical band (x*), zero at unison, near zero beyond about one critical band. Maximum of the curve is at df*s = ln(A2/A1)/(A2-A1) analytically; MEMORY.
- Vassilakis (2001/2005) roughness: R = (a1 a2)^0.1 * 0.5 (2 a2/(a1+a2))^3.11 * (exp(-3.5 s df) - exp(-5.75 s df)), with amplitude-dependent terms making roughness depend on modulation depth. MEMORY, not fetched.
- Sharpness (acum). S = 0.11 * sum N'(z) g(z) z dz / N, z in Bark grid 0.1..24, N' specific loudness (sone/Bark), N total loudness; set 0 if N < 0.1 sone. DIN 45692 g(z) = 1 for z <= 15.8; g(z) = 0.15 exp(0.42 (z - 15.8)) + 0.85 above. von Bismarck: g = 1 to 15 Bark, then 0.2 exp(0.308 (z-15)) + 0.8. Aures: g = 0.078 exp(0.171 z)/z * N/ln(0.05N+1). MoSQITo source (Eomys): https://github.com/Eomys/MoSQITo/blob/master/mosqito/sq_metrics/sharpness/sharpness_din/sharpness_din_from_loudness.py FETCHED. 1 acum = narrowband noise one critical band wide centred 1 kHz at 60 dB (MEMORY).
- Roughness (Daniel & Weber): total R = 0.25 * sum of specific roughnesses over 47 half-Bark channels; asper; frame 0.2 s overlap 0.5; input in Pa. MoSQITo roughness_dw.py. FETCHED. 1 asper = 1 kHz tone 60 dB, 100% AM at 70 Hz. Roughness peaks near 70 Hz modulation; fluctuation strength peaks near 4 Hz (1 vacil = 1 kHz, 60 dB, 100% AM at 4 Hz); above about 15-20 Hz modulation it becomes roughness, above about 150 Hz separate partials. Zwicker & Fastl, Psychoacoustics: Facts and Models. MEMORY.
- Schroeder spreading function (dB) over Bark difference dz = z_maskee - z_masker: SF = 15.81 + 7.5 (dz + 0.474) - 17.5 sqrt(1 + (dz + 0.474)^2). About -25 dB/Bark slope below masker, upward spread shallower (about -10 to -15 dB/Bark and level dependent). MEMORY.
- Audibility rule: per Bark band compare signal power to masker power. Noise masks tone when tone is within about 5-6 dB of the band noise (NMT offset 5.5 dB); a tone masks noise only when noise is about 14.5 + z dB below the tone masker (TMN offset 14.5 + z dB, Johnston/MPEG psychoacoustic model 1). Hawkins & Stevens give tone-in-noise threshold about -4 to -6 dB signal-to-noise in the critical band around 1 kHz. MEMORY. Practical flag: event is "likely masked" if its band SNR is below -6 dB in all bands that carry its energy; "clearly audible" above +6.
- Temporal masking: offset (post-) masking lasts about 100 ms, onset about 20 ms (article wording). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditory_masking FETCHED. Literature figure often quoted: forward masking 100-200 ms, backward 5-20 ms. MEMORY.
- Precedence / Haas: lags of 1-5 ms fuse for clicks, up to 40 ms for complex sounds; precedence range 2-50 ms; echo threshold about 50 ms speech, about 100 ms music; a reflection up to 10 dB louder than direct stays fused; Haas window 5-30 ms. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precedence_effect FETCHED.
- Attack time and "click": onsets under about 2-5 ms with broadband content are heard as clicks; 5-10 ms feels crisp, 20 ms+ soft. MEMORY (practice).

## 4. Music theory that can be checked

- Krumhansl-Kessler (essentia's krumhansl profile, tonic first): major 6.35 2.23 3.48 2.33 4.38 4.09 2.52 5.19 2.39 3.66 2.29 2.88; minor 6.33 2.68 3.52 5.38 2.60 3.53 2.54 4.75 3.98 2.69 3.34 3.17. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MTG/essentia/master/src/algorithms/tonal/key.cpp FETCHED. (Temperley revised: major 5.0 2.0 3.5 2.0 4.5 4.0 2.0 4.5 2.0 3.5 1.5 4.0; minor 5.0 2.0 3.5 4.5 2.0 4.0 2.0 4.5 3.5 2.0 1.5 4.0. FETCHED, same file.)
- Key detection and clarity (essentia): Pearson correlation of the pitch-class profile against the profile rotated through all 12 shifts for major and minor; key = best shift/scale, strength = max correlation; firstToSecondRelativeStrength = (max - previous max)/max. FETCHED (same). MIRtoolbox mirkey: same idea with key strength vector of 24 correlations; clarity as max or max minus mean. MEMORY. Typical: tonal music correlation above about 0.8, ambiguous below about 0.6 (MEMORY, rule of thumb).
- Spiral array tension (Chew; Herremans & Chew 2016): cloud diameter = largest distance between any two pitch positions in the current segment's spiral-array cloud (dissonance/spread); cloud momentum = distance between centres of effect of consecutive segments (how far harmony moves); tensile strain = distance from segment centre of effect to the key's centre (distance from tonic). MEMORY (the Wikipedia page did not cover it, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_array_model). Lerdahl 2001 Tonal Pitch Space: tension from hierarchical event tension plus surface (dissonance, inversion, melodic) tension; chord distance delta = i + j + k (regional, chordal, non-common-tone count in the basic space). MEMORY.
- Huron voice-leading (Tone and Voice, Music Perception 19(1) 1-64, 2001) derives rules from stream-segregation principles; search confirmed the six core principles and the derivation approach (SNIPPET) but not specifics. From memory: pitch proximity (prefer steps; stream integrity), pitch co-modulation, registral compass (limit to about 3-4 octaves per voice), avoid voice crossing and overlap, toneness (avoid tight clusters in low registers), spacing: adjacent upper voices within an octave, wider gaps allowed in the bass. MEMORY.
- Low interval limit (Plomp-Levelt consequence; Huron's Voice Leading 2016 ch. on masking): the gap between fundamentals must exceed about a critical band. Computable: using bandwidths above, a pair whose lower note is under about C3 (130 Hz) and gap under about 100 Hz makes partials beat. Customary orchestration rule: major/minor thirds below about C3 (minor thirds below E-flat 2-ish) are muddy, fifths acceptable down to about C2, octaves anywhere. Check by ERB(f) vs (f2 - f1). MEMORY, formula derived from fetched ERB/Bark tables.
- Narmour / Schellenberg: simplified implication-realisation, two principles: proximity (next interval small, 2 semitones or less fits best) and pitch reversal (after a large leap above about 5-6 semitones the next move should reverse direction, after small intervals continue). Schellenberg 1996/1997. MEMORY.
- Huron "Sweet Anticipation" 2006: ITPRA = Imagination, Tension, Prediction, Reaction, Appraisal; pleasure from prediction accuracy plus contrastive valence; measurable proxy: surprisal -log2 p(next event | context) under an n-gram or Markov model; melody entropy. MEMORY.
- Repetition and complexity: Berlyne inverted-U (Wundt curve) of liking vs arousal potential; Margulis, On Repeat (2014): repetition turns sound into music; stimuli looped feel musical (Deutsch speech-to-song). No numeric preferred complexity from memory beyond "medium". MEMORY.
- Groove: Witek et al. 2014, PLoS ONE 9(4):e94446: medium syncopation gives the most wanting-to-move and pleasure, inverted U; entropy was a poor predictor. SNIPPET (https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0094446, https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/syncopation-body-movement-and-pleasure-in-groove-music/). Their syncopation index is Longuet-Higgins and Lee style (weight differences on metrical grid): MEMORY.
- Microtiming: Fruhauf, Kopiez & Platz 2013, Musicae Scientiae 17(2):246-260: 93 music students, simple rock drum pattern; quantised version rated highest, larger deviations lowered ratings, early shifts worse than late, snare shifts mattered more than bass drum. SNIPPET (https://scite.ai and citing papers; doi 10.1177/1029864913486793). So default to quantised; add timing variation only as per-instrument humanisation of a few ms, and treat as unproven benefit.
- Swing: Friberg & Sundstrom 2002, Music Perception 19(3):333-349: swing ratio (long:short eighth) depends on tempo, about 3.5:1 at slow tempo toward 1:1 at fast; ride-cymbal ratios from about 3 at 120 bpm to about 1 at 300 bpm (quarter notes); roughly linear in tempo (disputed by Honing & de Haas). SNIPPET (secondary: https://jazztheory.ssac.carleton.ca/view.php?id=198, mcg.uva.nl honing-haas-2008.pdf). Ratio 2:1 (triplet) is a notation convention not the finding.
- Pulse clarity (Lartillot, Eerola, Toiviainen, Fornari 2008, ISMIR): features from autocorrelation of onset envelope (max peak height, peak contrast, entropy); modelled with ratings, r about 0.5-0.6 explained. MEMORY.
- Tempo by feel: ambient 60-80; ballad 60-80; hip-hop 80-100 (half-time feel at 140-160); house 118-128; techno 125-140; DnB 160-180; walking pace and preferred tempo about 100-120 bpm (Fraisse preferred tempo about 600 ms inter-onset). MEMORY.
- Phrase structure: 4-bar phrase, 8-bar period, 16 or 32-bar section; hypermetre in 4s; change something every 4/8 bars. Cadence tension scale (low to high resolution): authentic perfect (V-I root position) > imperfect > plagal > half (ends on V, open) > deceptive (V-vi). MEMORY.

## 5. Timbre and synthesis quality

- Grey 1977 (JASA 61(5)): timbre space dimensions: spectral energy distribution (brightness), attack synchrony/temporal evolution of upper harmonics, and spectral fine structure (spectral flux/irregularity). McAdams et al. 1995 add the attack time as a dimension in multi-instrument spaces; spectral centroid, attack time and spectral flux as the three robust dimensions. MEMORY.
- Risset & Mathews 1969 (trumpet analysis): upper-harmonic amplitude rises with overall level and the spectrum's partials' time courses differ, so loud notes are brighter; Chowning 1973 FM: modulation index envelope gives dynamic spectra with decaying brightness. MEMORY.
- Why raw oscillators sound cheap: static spectrum (no spectral flux); perfect harmonicity (real strings, bells inharmonic by B approx 1e-4 to 1e-3); no onset noise or transient; no inter-voice detune; vibrato onset delayed about 200-300 ms after note start; no note-to-note variation (same velocity, timing and phase). Checkable measures: spectral flux nonzero over sustain, partial-frequency variance across notes, attack envelope rise time, presence of noise component in first 20-50 ms. MEMORY (consensus of the sources above).
- Chorus/detune: 2-3 voices detuned +/-5 to 15 cents, LFO 0.1-1 Hz with 10-25 ms modulated delay. MEMORY practice.
- Register spacing: avoid more than one sustained voice in 200-500 Hz; thirds below about C3 muddy (see section 4).
- Reverb: small room RT60 0.3-0.6 s, medium hall 1.0-1.5, concert hall 1.5-2.5 s, cathedral 4+ s; early reflections 5-80 ms, pre-delay 10-30 ms, wet send around -18 to -12 dB under dry for sources that must stay intelligible; reverb returns high-passed above about 200-300 Hz and lowpassed around 8-10 kHz. MEMORY practice (consistent with the Haas window above for 10-30 ms pre-delay).
- Stereo: keep content below about 120 Hz mono; inter-channel correlation (phase correlation meter) staying above 0, ideally above about +0.3 averaged; mono-fold loss under 3 dB. MEMORY practice.

## 6. Sound design for film and games

- Murch, Dense Clarity - Clear Density (Transom): at most five layers (dialogue, music, linguistic effects, musical effects, mixed effects) can be tolerated if each is to be distinguished; this holds if layers spread over spectrum; if sounds stack in one spectral region the limit becomes two-and-a-half ("Law of Two-and-a-half"); six layers on Apocalypse Now cost clarity so he cut to five; encoded (language) and embodied (music) sound are processed differently so coexist. https://transom.org/2005/walter-murch/ FETCHED (first 100k characters).
- Chion (Audio-Vision, 1990): added value = sound enriches image so that the information seems to come from the image; synchresis = spontaneous fusing of sound and image with sync; causal listening = identify source, semantic listening = decode meaning, reduced listening = attend to sound traits themselves. MEMORY.
- Farnell, Designing Sound (MIT 2010) fire recipe: hiss (filtered noise, high frequency), crackle (sparse random impulses, clicks with random gain at Poisson rate), lapping/roar (low-passed noise with slow modulation). Rain: many random drops via noise bursts, pink/white mix with filter; wind: bandpass noise with slow random centre frequency (resonant, f Q-modulated); footsteps: heel-ball double impact from ground reaction force curve with two peaks (about 1.2 times body weight, 100-200 ms apart); explosions: initial crack plus low rumble plus debris tail. MEMORY, not fetched.
- Variation practice: SNIPPET from FMOD/UE5/Godot docs: pitch +/-8-10 percent multiplier (UE5 guide range 0.92-1.08), volume scalar 0.85-1.0 (about -1.4 to 0 dB), Godot randomiser volume offset in dB (+/-3 dB example) and pitch in semitones; at least 3 distinct footstep files per surface (FMOD tutorial, https://gamedevacademy.org/hey-listen-sound-design-with-unity-fmod-part-2/). Larger pitch shifts sound like different creatures; volume variation should be smaller than pitch variation. MEMORY adds: usual 4-8 variants plus round-robin without immediate repeat; pitch +/-100 cents.
- Impact layering (transient, body, tail, sweetener): MEMORY practice; transient 1-10 ms click or slap, body 50-200 ms, tail decays with room, sweetener optional. Standard.
- HDR audio, DICE (Battlefield: Bad Company, GDC 2009, Clerwall): loudest current sound sets the window top; a sliding window of about 40 dB below is audible, quieter sounds are culled/dropped. GDC talk exists (https://ea.com/frostbite/news/how-hdr-audio-makes-battlefield-bad-company-go-boom); numbers not retrieved. SNIPPET for existence, MEMORY for 40 dB.
- Ducking: music ducked 3-6 dB under dialogue, attack 50-150 ms, hold, release 0.5-1.5 s; sidechain compression ratios 3:1-6:1. MEMORY practice. Audiokinetic foley case study mentions ducking the body loop bus per footstep (no numbers) SNIPPET.
- Category balance: dialogue loudest, music 6-12 dB under SFX in action, ambience another 6-10 dB under that; footsteps set about 6-12 dB under dialogue and ducked by louder events. MEMORY practice; no source gave numbers (designingsound loudness article had none).
- Fatigue: repetition at identical timbre plus 3-8 kHz excess are the main fatigue drivers; high-frequency heavy repeated sounds (footsteps, UI clicks) should be low-passed or levelled low; vary at least pitch and sample. MEMORY.
- Adaptive music: vertical layering (stems faded by game state), horizontal resequencing (segments joined at beat/bar boundaries via transitions), stingers on musical grid; iMUSE (LucasArts 1991) invented transitions at marked points. MEMORY.
