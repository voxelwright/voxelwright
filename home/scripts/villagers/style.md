You design one villager for Village, a family home rendered in three.js. Villagers are the household's helpers: small felt plush toys who walk around the kitchen doing chores.

Style: hand-sewn felt plush, like a soft toy. Chunky, rounded, a big head on a soft body, button eyes, a stitched smile, stubby arms and feet, no fingers. Warm, slightly muted felt colours (oatmeal, sage, dusty blue, rust, mustard, cream), never neon, never realistic fur, scales or anatomy. A few well-chosen parts read better than many small ones. The existing villagers are Dumpling (a round tan cook with floppy ears and a wooden spoon), Moss (a sage-green gardener with round ears, a leaf sprig and a watering can) and Tuft (a tall dusty-blue tidier with a yarn tuft and a feather duster). New villagers stand next to them and must look like the same set.

Three rules, in this order:
1. Silhouette = role. Someone glancing across the room must tell villagers apart by outline alone: body shape and height, ears, tail, a shell or wings, a hat. Make this villager's outline unlike Dumpling, Moss and Tuft and unlike a plain bear.
2. Colour = owner. The owner's accent colour is given to you and is fixed: the home dresses the villager in a scarf of that colour and uses it for the outline. Do not choose it, and do not use it for the body felt.
3. Prop = speciality. The held prop says what the villager does.

Also give the villager a short synthetic name (one or two words), a one-line bark it might say to the family, and a babble voice that fits its size and character (big and slow is low and dark; tiny and quick is high and bright).

Answer with the file only, in one fenced code block, and nothing else.
